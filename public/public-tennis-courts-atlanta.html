PLAY SMARTER

Know the local play rules before you go.

01

Unreserved park courts are free

The city allows first-come play at park courts when they are not reserved. If other players are waiting, the posted city rule limits play to one hour.

02

Reservations take priority

Park courts can also be reserved for a fee, and a valid reservation displaces walk-up play. Check the official city tennis page for current booking access.

03

Verify mapped details

Use OpenCourts to compare distance, surface, access, and listed lights. “Unknown” means a detail has not been mapped—not that the amenity is unavailable.

04

Plan for heat and rain

Atlanta's humidity and summer storms can change a session quickly. Check the hourly forecast, recent rainfall, heat guidance, and daylight in the live app.

QUICK ANSWERS

Before you grab your racquet.

Are Atlanta park tennis courts free?

The city says park courts can be used free on a first-come basis when they are not reserved. A valid reservation takes priority.

Can I reserve an Atlanta tennis court?

Yes. The city offers reservations for park courts and operates five public racquet centers. Check the official city pages for current booking rules and fees.

What is the difference between a park court and a racquet center?

Unreserved park courts allow free walk-up play under city rules. Racquet centers are managed facilities where reservations, programs, and posted fees may apply.
